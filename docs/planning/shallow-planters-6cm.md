# Two shallow planters — 6 cm substrate

This plan applies to the two physical containers [`planter_85x15_01`](../containers/planter-85x15-01.md) and [`planter_85x15_02`](../containers/planter-85x15-02.md).

## Assumptions

- 2 planters: **85 × 15 cm**.
- Substrate depth: **6 cm**.
- Each planter is divided into **5 logical modules of approximately 17 × 15 cm**.
- Total: **10 modules, A–J**.
- The planters are elevated.
- Their long axes run **N–S** in the current layout; primary light arrives from **W**.
- The balcony door separates the planters, so the plan assumes they do not shade each other.
- Operational priority: **convenience, few different handling patterns, direct sowing, and as much cut-and-come-again harvesting as practical**.
- Weeks are written as **wwXX** using ISO week numbers; the number marks the start week of that crop.
- For winter crops, starting early enough is more important than extracting the final possible summer harvest.

Module letters are planting-plan labels only; they are not physical container IDs.

Use the [interactive planting calendar](../calendar.md) for upcoming transition windows. The calendar derives a final-harvest/clearing event from each rotation boundary: the outgoing crop is finished immediately before the incoming crop is started in the same ISO week. No exact day is implied.

## Repeatable blocks

- **A, B, D:** Klajtonia → Portulaka warzywna.
- **C, J:** Roszponka → Portulaka warzywna.
- **F, G:** Pietruszka naciowa → Klajtonia.

The repetition is intentional: it reduces the number of distinct sowing, watering and harvesting routines.

## Autumn changeover

- **Roszponka:** standard start around **ww36**.
- **Klajtonia:** standard start around **ww38**.

A summer crop may therefore be ended while it is still producing if that is needed to give the winter crop enough development before very short days.

## Crop handling rules

### Klajtonia

**3×3 = 9 clumps, 2–3 seeds/point, 5–6 mm deep → keep 1–2 strongest**

Harvest:

- autumn and spring can be harvested more assertively;
- in winter, prefer individual large leaves;
- if cutting an entire clump, leave approximately **2–3 cm above the soil** and do not damage the growth centre;
- at the end of the crop phase, a full harvest can be taken and the roots removed.

### Portulaka warzywna

**3×3 = 9 clumps, 2–4 seeds/point, 2–4 mm deep → keep 1–2 strongest**

Harvest:

- handle similarly to basil by regularly shortening young shoot tips;
- normally harvest the terminal **4–7 cm of shoot**;
- leave several leaves/nodes and some side branching below the cut;
- do not remove all green mass in one harvest;
- at the end of the season, take a full harvest and clear the module for the winter crop.

### Roszponka

**3×3 = 9 plants, 2–3 seeds/point, 5–6 mm deep → keep 1 strongest**

Harvest:

- primarily take the largest **outer leaves**, keeping the centre of the rosette;
- harvest more conservatively in winter;
- cut the full rosette mainly at the final harvest or when a larger one-off harvest is needed.

### Pietruszka naciowa

The layout has already been practically tested:

**2×2 = 4 plants per module**

Other sowing and harvest parameters remain to be defined.

## Decisions

- **Szczypiorek and cebula siedmiolatka** were removed from these shallow planters; the plan treats deeper containers as the better place for those perennial crops.
- **Szpinak** was removed. The plan rejected both a dense baby-leaf cycle requiring fuller harvests and reseeding, and a sparser approach where 6 cm substrate becomes a stronger limitation.
- **Amarantus baby leaf** was removed for a similar reason: the plan considered it better suited either to a short dense baby-leaf crop or to a larger plant with more root volume.
- **Mizuna** was removed. It was considered technically suitable, but seed would be bought specifically for only 1–2 modules and the taste is not yet known.
- **Portulaka warzywna** is the main summer crop in suitable modules because the plan values its fit with heat, shallow substrate and repeated harvesting.
- **Roszponka and klajtonia** are the main autumn–winter–spring crops.
- **Klajtonia** is preferred where strong late-winter and spring regrowth matters.
- **Roszponka** is preferred where a classic winter rosette and strong cold tolerance matter.
- A larger number of winter roszponka modules is acceptable.
- **Zellarosa** remains because the seed is already purchased, despite not being ideal for a very shallow container.
- **Królowa Majowych** remains as a separate spring module.

## Layout and convenience

With the planter axes N–S and light mainly from W, the plan treats height differences between adjacent modules as relatively unimportant. The layout is optimized primarily for **ease of handling and repeatable rotations**, not classic companion planting.

During mid-summer, many modules switch to portulaka at the same time, simplifying:

- watering;
- harvest technique;
- fertilization;
- crop monitoring.
