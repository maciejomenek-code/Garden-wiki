# Bazylia

This page stores reusable guidance for preparing a container and substrate for basil. It does **not** assign basil to any specific pot.

## Container

Recommended minimum substrate depth: **15–20 cm**.

For the current pot inventory:

- a **Ø19 × 19 cm** pot is suitable for one ordinary basil plant;
- a **Ø24 × 24 cm** pot gives one plant substantially more root volume and water reserve, so it is the preferred option for vigorous growth and frequent harvesting.

The pot must have drainage holes in the bottom.

## Substrate

A practical starting mix is:

- **70%** good-quality universal potting soil;
- **20%** mature compost;
- **10%** perlite.

If the potting soil is already light and contains enough aerating material such as perlite or coconut fibre, use approximately:

- **75%** potting soil;
- **25%** mature compost.

Target pH: approximately **6.0–7.0**.

The substrate should be fertile and moisture-retentive while still draining excess water readily.

## Preparing the pot

1. Make sure the bottom has several unobstructed drainage holes.
2. Do **not** add a thick drainage layer of stones or expanded clay. It reduces useful root volume; good drainage should come primarily from the substrate and drainage holes.
3. If necessary, place a small piece of mesh over the drainage holes to limit substrate loss.
4. Mix the potting soil, compost and perlite before filling the pot.
5. Fill the pot while leaving approximately **2–3 cm** below the rim for watering.
6. Settle the substrate lightly by hand, but do not compact it.
7. Water thoroughly before sowing or transplanting, until a small amount of water drains from the bottom.

## Preferred setup for intensive harvest

For a basil plant intended for frequent harvesting through the season, the preferred setup is:

**Ø24 × 24 cm pot + 70% potting soil / 20% compost / 10% perlite.**

This balances nutrient supply, water retention and root aeration.
